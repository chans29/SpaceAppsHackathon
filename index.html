Basic Stretches

Stretching can make you more flexible and help your joints move through their full range. Here are the main points from Mayo Clinic's guide.

Stretch safely

  • Warm up first with 5 to 10 minutes of light activity, like walking. Stretching after a workout is usually best.
  • Move slowly and gently. Don't bounce, and keep breathing.
  • Stretch until you feel a slight pull, then hold for about 30 seconds.
  • Do each stretch 2 to 4 times on each side.
  • Aim for at least 2 to 3 days a week.
  • Pain means you've gone too far, so ease off.
  • If you have an injury or health condition, ask a doctor or physical therapist which stretches are right for you.

Calf stretch

Stand an arm's length from a wall with one foot behind the other. Bend your front knee forward, keeping your back leg straight and its heel on the floor.

Hamstring stretch

Lie on the floor next to a doorway or wall. Rest one heel up on the wall with the knee slightly bent, then slowly straighten that leg.

Quadriceps stretch

Hold a wall for balance. Grab one ankle and gently pull your heel up behind you. Keep your knees together and your stomach tight.

Hip flexor stretch

Kneel on one knee, with a folded towel under it, and put the other foot in front of you. Keep your back straight and lean your hips forward.

Outer thigh (IT band) stretch

Stand beside a wall for support. Cross one ankle over the other, then reach that same arm up and over toward the opposite side.

Knee-to-chest stretch

Lie on your back and gently pull one knee toward your chest until you feel it in your lower back. Skip this one if you have osteoporosis.

Shoulder stretch

Bring one arm across your body. Use your other arm to hold it just above or below the elbow.

Shoulder stretch with towel

Hold a rolled towel behind your back with one hand above your head and one low. Pull up with the top hand so the lower hand slides up your back.

Neck stretch

Tilt your head forward and a little to one side. Use the hand on that side to gently pull your head down.

Source: Mayo Clinic Staff, “A guide to basic stretches,” mayoclinic.org. Summarized here; see the full guide for pictures and complete steps.
